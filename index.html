Tooling & Mold Design Engineering

Your machining team is specifying the injection tooling for a 500ml PET water bottle. The choices involve high-precision P20 pre-hardened tool steel with full Moldflow simulation versus fast-machined tooling. Mold precision determines shrinkage rates, parting line flash, and uniform wall thickness.

Quality Engineering Principle: 80% of part defects are locked in during the design and tooling stage. Prevention investments here yield 10x savings in post-molding scrap.

Cost of Quality (CoQ) & Engineering Principles

1. The 1-10-100 Rule of Quality Costs

Spending $1 on prevention (QA mold FEA simulation) prevents $10 of internal inspection/scrap correction in the lab, and avoids $100+ of catastrophic external recall costs once the bottle hits consumer hands.

Prevention Costs (QA)

Actions taken upstream to design and build quality into the process (Tooling precision, mold thermal balancing, SPC calibration).

Appraisal Costs (QC)

Inspecting and measuring downstream items to catch non-conformances (Leak decay testing, vision gauges, burst testing).

Internal Failures

Scrap, machine downtime, purging bad resin, and regrinding defective bottles before customer shipment.

External Failures

Leaking bottles on store shelves, brand PR disaster, warranty claims, retailer fines, and legal recalls.

Important Lab Insight: Overspending on extreme prevention without considering cycle time will push the manufacturing cost per unit too high, causing uncompetitive unit economics even if defects are 0%.
